# Risk Management Basics

## Why Risk Management Comes First

You can have a terrible win rate and still be profitable — if your winners are much larger than your losers. Conversely, a high win rate means nothing if one bad trade wipes out months of gains. Risk management is the foundation everything else rests on.

## Core Concepts

### Risk-Reward Ratio (RRR)

Before entering any trade, define your target and stop loss.

```
RRR = (Target Price - Entry Price) / (Entry Price - Stop Loss)
Minimum acceptable RRR: 2:1  (risk $1 to make $2)
```

### Position Sizing

Decide how many shares/contracts to buy based on your dollar risk tolerance, not a fixed number of shares.

```
Position Size = Dollar Risk Per Trade / (Entry Price - Stop Price)
Example: Risk $200, stop is $2 away → buy 100 shares
```

### Max Drawdown Awareness

Track your peak-to-trough equity decline. Larger drawdowns require larger percentage gains just to break even:

```
10% loss → need 11.1% gain to recover
25% loss → need 33.3% gain to recover
50% loss → need 100% gain to recover
```

### Expectancy

The average amount you expect to win per dollar risked over many trades.

```
Expectancy = (Win Rate × Avg Win) – (Loss Rate × Avg Loss)
Positive expectancy = edge over time
```

## Practical Rules

- Always set a stop loss *before* entering the trade.
- Never move a stop further away when it is about to be hit.
- Scale out of winners; do not add to losers.
- Cut losses quickly, let winners run.
- Keep a trading journal — track every trade with rationale and outcome.

> [!WARNING]
> **The cardinal sin:** Adding to a losing position hoping it will "come back." This turns a manageable loss into a potentially account-ending one.

## Emotional Risk

Emotional decisions are the biggest risk. After a large loss, the urge to "revenge trade" is strong — and almost always makes things worse. Recognize the emotional state and take a break.

> [!TIP]
> If you feel emotional about a trade (fear, excitement, greed), that is a signal to reduce size or step away — not to press harder.
