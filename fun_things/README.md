# 🎲 Monte Carlo Markets Lab

An interactive **Geometric Brownian Motion** simulator that fans out thousands of possible
price futures for a stock and reads off the statistics a quant actually cares about —
**probability of profit**, **95% Value-at-Risk**, and the **percentile fan (P5 / P25 / P50 / P75 / P95)**.

👉 **Live:** [`/fun_things/`](./index.html) &nbsp;·&nbsp; single file, **zero dependencies**, pure `<canvas>`.

## What it does

Every faint line is one simulated future generated by the model banks use to price options:

```
S(t+Δt) = S(t) · exp[ (μ − ½σ²)·Δt + σ·√Δt · Z ],   Z ~ 𝒩(0,1),   Δt = 1/252
```

- **μ** — annualized drift (expected return)
- **σ** — annualized volatility
- **Z** — a standard-normal shock, drawn with the **Box–Muller** transform

Run it a few hundred times and you get the *distribution* of where the price could land —
which is the whole point: markets aren't a single forecast, they're a cone of probabilities.

## Things to try

- **🏦 Blue Chip vs 🚀 Meme Stock** — watch the cone explode as σ jumps from 18% → 90%.
- **🐻 Bear Market** — negative drift; notice probability-of-profit drop below 50% even though *some* paths still moon.
- Crank **volatility** and watch the **median** (typical outcome) fall *below* the **mean** — that gap is the log-normal skew that makes lotteries and meme stocks feel exciting but lose on average.

## What it demonstrates

- **Quant finance** — GBM, risk-neutral drift adjustment (the `−½σ²` Itô term), VaR, percentile bands
- **Numerical methods** — Monte Carlo simulation, Box–Muller sampling, interpolated quantiles
- **Front-end engineering** — retina-aware `<canvas>`, `requestAnimationFrame` animation, responsive layout, **no libraries**
